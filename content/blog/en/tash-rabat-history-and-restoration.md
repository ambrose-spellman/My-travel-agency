Tash-Rabat is a stone caravanserai tucked into a quiet side valley in the Naryn region, at around 3,200 m — one of the best-preserved historic buildings in Kyrgyzstan and a rare stop on the old Silk Road that you can still walk through today.

The site has been studied for nearly a century. An expedition led by B. M. Ziman surveyed it in 1937, followed by the Tien-Shan-Alai archaeological expedition under A. Bernshtam in 1944. A three-year architectural and archaeological study between 1978 and 1980 gave researchers a much fuller picture of the building's structure, and a restoration of the site — now a state-protected cultural monument — was completed in 1980.

Important if you're planning a visit: as of 2023, Tash-Rabat is undergoing a new round of restoration and conservation work under the Kyrgyz government's heritage program for 2023-2027 (approved by Cabinet of Ministers decree No. 701, 22 December 2023). The goal is to stabilize the structure and make it safe for future visitors.

While work is underway, some areas of the complex are temporarily fenced off, and the Ministry of Culture, Information and Youth Policy has asked visitors to hold off on visiting until the main stages of restoration are finished. The restrictions are temporary and are about safety and protecting the monument, not a permanent closure.
