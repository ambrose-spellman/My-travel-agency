Issyk-Kul

The great lake, red canyons and the gateway to the Tien Shan

About Issyk-Kul

Issyk-Kul is one of the largest alpine lakes in the world, ringed by the Kungey and Terskey Ala-Too ranges. Along its shores are red-rock canyons, waterfalls and the town of Karakol — a relaxed add-on to a mountain trip or a destination in its own right.

Best timeApril–October
From Bishkek4–5 hours by car
ActivitiesSwimming, hiking, sightseeing
DifficultyEasy

Highlights

Skazka (Fairy Tale) Canyon
Wind-carved red sandstone ridges on the lake's southern shore, best explored in the late afternoon light. An easy walk suitable for all ages.
Jety-Oguz
The 'Seven Bulls' — a row of red sandstone cliffs near Karakol, with a short, scenic valley walk continuing up toward alpine meadows and hot springs.
Karakol
The region's main town, with a wooden Russian Orthodox cathedral, a lively Sunday animal market nearby, and easy access to the Terskey Ala-Too for further trekking.

Getting There

Issyk-Kul is reached via the Bishkek-Balykchy road, continuing along either the northern (Cholpon-Ata) or southern (Bokonbaevo) shore.

• Private car transfer from Bishkek (included in tours)
• Shared taxi or marshrutka from Bishkek's Vostok-5 station
• Northern shore is faster; southern shore is quieter and more scenic
